RESEARCH BRIEF — LTO Strategy in Regional Burger Chains (2026)

CLIENT
Mid-tier protein supplier evaluating how to position a new premium chicken
patty SKU into the regional (50–300 unit) burger chain segment. The client
currently over-indexes in fine dining and independent operators; they have
limited visibility into how regional chains actually make LTO (limited-time
offer) decisions.

OBJECTIVES
1. Understand how regional burger chains decide what becomes an LTO vs. a
   permanent menu addition in 2026.
2. Map the real decision-makers — culinary R&D, operations, procurement,
   franchisee councils — and their veto points.
3. Identify which levers a supplier can actually pull to win a slot
   (sample-first? 12-month price lock? competitive intel?) vs. the rituals
   that feel productive but don't move the needle.
4. Surface the back-of-house constraints (labor, equipment, prep steps) that
   silently kill otherwise good LTO concepts before they reach the menu.

TOPIC CONTEXT
The broader category has been under sustained labor pressure since 2023.
Operators have publicly stated they are designing new items around a
"two-step prep max" constraint. Several chains have consolidated distributors
in the last 18 months, which has shortened the list of suppliers who can
realistically win a national rollout.

At the same time, premium chicken as a category has grown ~14% YoY in
regional burger QSR while beef LTO mix has plateaued. The client's thesis
is that there is white space for a premium chicken patty positioned as a
beef-LTO ALTERNATIVE rather than a chicken-category entrant — but they want
to test that thesis against operator reality before committing tooling.

TARGET EXPERTS (5–20 interviews across these profiles)
- Directors/VPs of culinary at regional burger chains (50–300 units)
- Menu strategy leads at chain-adjacent consultancies
- Regional distributor category managers (Sysco, US Foods, PFG regional arms)
- Franchisee council leadership at 3+ chains
- Protein brokers who sell into this segment

KEY QUESTIONS TO ANSWER
- What is the real decision loop for a 4–8 week LTO in a chain this size?
  How compressed is the timeline from concept to rollout?
- What kills an LTO before it launches? Rank order: labor, equipment,
  pricing, supply continuity, marketing fit, franchisee resistance.
- What does a winning supplier pitch look like in 2026? Is the "deck + rep
  visit" model still functional, or has it been displaced?
- How much do operators actually weight competitive intelligence ("chain X is
  running your product") vs. their own sampling?
- What is the right price-lock horizon to offer — 6, 12, 18 months? Where
  does the operator's own distributor contract renegotiation cycle fall?

OUT OF SCOPE
- Fast-casual and fine-dining segments (covered in prior engagement).
- International markets.
- Plant-based protein alternatives.

DELIVERABLE
A decision-grade set of 4–8 themes with supporting verbatim quotes, a
ranked list of objections the supplier will face, and a recommendation on
the sample-first vs. deck-first go-to-market motion.
